Vertical 9:16 cinematic key art for GUTTER CITY — the street-crew drama of the game GUTTERCAPS. Style: semi-realistic painted graphic-novel realism, thick graphic shadows, subtle halftone, spray-paint grit, wet surfaces, volumetric haze, heavy 35 mm grain, low-key cinematic lighting; soaked night, rain. Palette: asphalt black #16151A–#2A2830 with all district accents used sparingly (cyan #16E5D9, orange #FF7A1A, magenta #FF2E8A, ice blue #9AD9FF, acid #B6FF3C). Camera: wide eye level down the length of one street — a vertical corridor — hero crews in the middle and lower thirds, dark uncluttered top third (title space), dark wet bottom third (subtitle space). Mood: gangster chill — laid-back, confident, quiet power; nobody running, nobody shouting, no fighting; a whole city agreeing not to start anything tonight. The image is about people, never about objects: no bottle caps as the hero of the frame. No readable text anywhere — graffiti, neon, patches and labels are abstract shapes. No logos, no real brands, no celebrity likenesses, no coins or banknotes, no weapons aimed at people. Not cartoonish, not anime, not flat vector, not pastel, not daylight.

SCENE — one rainy street under the elevated tracks, the promo poster of the city, everyone in their own corner of the same night: MOTH, the masked stencil legend, crouched on a scaffold edge finishing a cyan moth piece; SPOKE with his cast and BMX sitting on a concrete beam while his crew rides a flat bar below; RAIL in a hi-vis vest leaning on a chain-link fence with two writers and their cans; RUSTY STITCH at his little stall with the kettle and racks of sneakers; MUTE with the crew on a stoop and the boombox with its cable running up the lamp post; BEASTKEEPER with the raccoons and the one-eyed cat by the dumpster; PLAYER ONE sitting on the bumper of a beat-up van whose open door reveals an arcade cabinet; THE CHRONICLER up on the fire escape with the typewriter and the crows. Rain, steam, puddles, neon blobs, wet asphalt mirroring every colour, layers stacked roof → street → drain.
